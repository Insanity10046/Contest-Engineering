# The Justification of the Field's Methods

*Status: Draft v0.1. This document answers the question: "Why do we use these specific methods, in this specific order, and how do they produce valid knowledge?"*

## 1. The Central Methodology: Warrant-Chain Engineering
Contest Engineering does not use the linear "hypothesis testing" of the scientific method, nor the "build-measure-learn" of software startups. It uses **Warrant-Chain Engineering**.
*   **The Logic:** Every stage of the lifecycle is a claim (a warrant) that must be defended before proceeding. If the warrant fails, the process loops back. 
*   **The Justification:** Because contests involve a responding adversary, you cannot make a single "final" assessment. You must continuously validate your standing, your reading of the situation, your adversary model, and your rule interpretation.

## 2. Methodological Pluralism (Why the 10-Step Process?)
No single donor discipline can model a hybrid contest. The methodology borrows methods based on their **terrain-agnosticism** and **auditability**:

1.  **EWMR (Frame):** Because objectives and authority cannot be assumed; they must be formalized before design begins.
2.  **The Intelligence Cycle (Survey):** Because the baseline must be empirically observed and refreshed, not guessed.
3.  **Level-k Modeling (Model Adversary):** Because mirror-imaging is the field's primary cognitive failure mode. Level-k forces us to model how deeply the adversary reasons about us.
4.  **Doctrinal Analysis (Analyze Rules):** Because rules are not just text; they are interpretive systems. A formal model that gets the interpretation wrong is a failure.
5.  **Formalism Triage (Select Representation):** Because the choice of diagram dictates what you can see and what you are blind to. 
6.  **Extensive-Form Modeling (Model Contest):** Because it is the only formalism that can express *order*, *information sets*, and *strategy* simultaneously.
7.  **Pattern Language (Design Architecture):** Because architecture requires composing five interdependent components, not just tweaking one rule.
8.  **Wargaming (Stress-Test):** Because the only way to test an architecture is against an opponent who designs back.
9.  **OODA (Conduct):** Because during execution, the clock speed of adaptation determines survival.
10. **After-Action Review (Review):** Because the trace and the precedent are the field's institutional memory.

## 3. The Epistemic / Operational Methodological Split
The methodology mandates a strict boundary between simulation and execution:
*   **Open Simulation:** Modeling, wargaming, and publishing adversary models are methodologically **open**. The field's validity depends on rigorous, peer-reviewed modeling of hostile actors.
*   **Gated Deployment:** Conduct is methodologically **gated**. An architecture cannot cross from the stress-test to the conduct phase without passing the Assurance Gate. The methodology requires that authority is established *externally* and *before* execution.

## 4. Why Methodology is Separate from Process
*   The **Process Model** (`process.md`) tells the practitioner *what* to do (Step 1, Step 2, etc.).
*   The **Methodology** (this document) tells the field *why* those steps are valid. 
*   If the process model is the codebase, the methodology is the test suite that proves the codebase isn't just making things up. 

## 5. The Native Method Problem (Open)
The methodology acknowledges that every method currently used is *borrowed*. The field has a distinct process (Warrant-Chain), but no native method. 
*   **Research Agenda:** The field must invent a native method, most likely at **Step 6 (Model Contest)** or **Step 7 (Design Architecture)**. Until then, the field's methodology relies on the rigorous synthesis of donor methods rather than its own original formalism.
