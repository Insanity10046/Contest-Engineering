# The Epistemology of Contest Engineering

*Status: Draft v0.1. This document answers the question: "What counts as knowledge in this field, and what is the nature of the thing we study?"*

## 1. The Ontological Premise: Contests are Constructed
A contest is not a natural phenomenon that we merely observe; it is a **designed system**. Even when a contest is inherited (a war, a lawsuit, a market entry), its rules, information flows, physical affordances, and trace environments were built by someone, or evolved through structural incentives. 
*   **Axiom:** The structure of a contest determines its outcome more than the moves made within it.
*   **Consequence:** Contest engineering is not a social science. It is a design discipline. We do not seek to merely describe the contest; we seek to intervene in its architecture.

## 2. The Epistemic Stance: Architectural Realism
We reject two extremes: the belief that contests are purely subjective (social constructionism) and the belief that they are perfectly deterministic (pure rational-actor modeling).
*   **Constructed Constraints:** We treat the environment (rules, information, physical space, traces) as objectively real constraints that shape behavior, but we recognize that these constraints are *human-made* and therefore malleable.
*   **Bounded Rationality:** We assume adversaries are boundedly rational. They use heuristics, suffer from cognitive biases, operate within organizational friction, and act on incomplete information. 
*   **Reflexivity:** We assume the adversary is observing us. Knowledge of the adversary is not a static fact; it is a co-evolutionary loop. 

## 3. The Definition of "Truth" (Validity)
In traditional science, a claim is true if it is empirically verified. In engineering, a claim is true if the artifact works. In Contest Engineering, "working" is defined as:
*   **Constraint, not Determination:** We never claim to "know" exactly what an adversary will do. We claim to know the *distribution* of their possible actions and how our architecture shifts that distribution.
*   **Falsifiability via Wargaming:** An architecture is only "known" to be valid if it survives stress-testing against a responding adversary. An untested architecture is a hypothesis, not knowledge.
*   **Trace Legibility:** A contest is only understood retrospectively through the traces it leaves. Knowledge of what happened is always an exercise in evidence analysis and attribution.

## 4. The Ethical Epistemology: Epistemic vs. Operational
The field draws a hard line between *knowing* and *doing*.
*   **Epistemic Work:** Modeling bad actors, simulating criminal behavior, and red-teaming are not just permitted—they are *epistemically mandatory*. You cannot engineer a constraint against a decision process you refuse to model.
*   **Operational Work:** Applying an architecture to a non-consenting party is not an act of knowing; it is an act of power. It requires external lawful authority, not just epistemic validity.
