# Contest-Engineering
> Contest engineering is the discipline that designs, analyzes, and assures adversarial multi-domain environments — rules, information flows, physical affordances, trace and evidence structures, and strategic sequencing — such that an adaptive adversary's bounded decision processes are constrained toward intended outcomes under lawful, ethical, and measurable constraints.

# PROBLEM STATEMENT
Contests like battles, litigations, elections, negotiations, market entries, cyber intrusions, regulatory fights are increasingly multi-domain. The parties who win are rarely those who fight best inside a given domain; they are those who shape the domain itself: the rules that will be applied, the information that will be believed, the physical and procedural space in which the opponent must act, and the record that will survive afterward.

Existing fields each own one slice of this. Game theory and mechanism design model rule effects but are thin on perception, evidence, and physical affordance. Security engineering builds robust systems but assumes a fixed adversary and a fixed environment. Legal studies analyze rules but do not treat rule design as an engineering discipline with measurable outcomes. Forensic science reads traces but rarely treats trace production as a design variable. Environmental criminology and CPTED shape space but not multi-domain sequencing. Military operational design sequences campaigns but is not generalized to legal, market, or informational contests. Intelligence studies describe tradecraft but not its engineering constraints.

No field currently treats the contest architecture as a single coupled design object with methods, metrics, and assurance. Contest Engineering exists to fill that gap. If it does not produce methods and benchmarks that these fields lack, it has no reason to exist and should be dissolved into them.

# ENGINEERED ARTIFACT
The design object of the field is the **contest architecture**, defined as the coupled configuration of five components:
1. Rule architecture — procedures, constraints, enforcement, incentives, jurisdiction, standing, thresholds.
2. Information architecture — what is knowable, by whom, when; signaling, disclosure, secrecy, narrative, deception.
3. Physical and procedural architecture — space, access, chokepoints, visibility, tempo, interface and choice architecture.
4. Trace architecture — what records are produced, who can read them, how they are interpreted, how attribution works.
5. Sequencing architecture — the order and timing of moves across domains and phases (before / during / after).

Contest Engineering's technical claim is that these five are coupled: changing one changes the others, and treating them separately produces predictable failure. The field's methods exist to model, design, and validate that coupling.
